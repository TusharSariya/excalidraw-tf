## Findings

1. **[P1] The shipped §2.5 harness labels a p90 gate but bootstraps the mean.** v3.0 requires slice-B p50 and p90 gates, and v3.1 says p90 is gated when `nB ≥ 30` ([v3.0 §2](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/docs/rcll-v2-spec-v3.md:54), [v3.1 §2.5](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/docs/rcll-v2-spec-v3.1.md:53)). The implementation explicitly computes a CI “on the mean paired delta”; `bootstrapGatePolicy()` merely returns the label `"p90"` and never changes the bootstrapped statistic ([terraformPipelineBootstrapCi.ts](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/packages/excalidraw/components/terraformPipelineBootstrapCi.ts:121), [extent harness](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/packages/excalidraw/components/terraformPipelineStrataExtentGate.test.ts:23)). W4 consequently declares parity from mean Δ `−112 [−956,+784]` while p90 still regresses `+2068px` ([W4 §2](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/docs/strata-view-w4-extent-report.md:48)). This reintroduces the exact mean/Σ-vs-tail defect round 7 intended to eliminate. The strict-pass chase is currently testing the wrong statistic.

2. **[P1] The roadmap optimizes static layout proxies while the primary task is interactive impact tracing.** The declared task is downstream/upstream tracing ([v3.1 front matter](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/docs/rcll-v2-spec-v3.1.md:8)), but the only task validation scheduled is a 20-edge hand-label of whether X reads as dataflow direction; no completion record exists after SDEC-24 ([decision log](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/docs/strata-view-decision-log.md:87)). Reproducible source search—`rg -i "impact[ -]?trac|dependency[ -]?highlight|highlight.*(upstream|downstream)" packages/excalidraw excalidraw-app`—returns nothing. Empirically, Ware et al. found shortest-path time predicted by path continuity, crossings on the target path, and branches—not total graph crossings or raw geometric length; continuity was the strongest layout aesthetic after intrinsic path length ([Ware et al. 2002](https://vislab-ccom.unh.edu/pdfs/GraphAesthetics.pdf)). Huang’s path task went from median 6.81s without crossings to 14.74s with near-right-angle crossings and 29.41s with acute crossings; all pairwise differences were significant ([Huang 2008](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.4431)). Accurate path/subgraph highlighting was significantly faster and less error-prone than no highlighting in a controlled 32-participant path-tracing study ([Dawson et al. 2011](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~jqdawson/publications/EphPathTR_Dawson_2011.pdf)). Layout is a substrate, not the highest-leverage unfinished feature for this task.

3. **[P1] Global layered compound layout is grounded; full-width provider/account bands are not.** Sander/Forster support global base-node ranks, derived cluster spans, and hierarchy constraints. Forster explicitly demonstrates that independently laying out hierarchy levels—top-down or bottom-up—creates avoidable crossings; his remedy operates on global layers with contiguity and consistent-relative-order constraints (`forster-compound-crossing-gd2002`, pp. 2–6). ELK similarly reports that independent bottom-up ordering creates preventable hierarchical-edge crossings and requires hierarchy-aware ordering to fix them (`s2-10-48550-arxiv-2311-00533`, p. 6). Strata instead makes provider/account children full-width sequential bands ([v2.0 A0](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/docs/rcll-v2-spec-v2.md:189)); its weighted skipped-band objective is admitted to be unprecedented ([v3.1 §0](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/docs/rcll-v2-spec-v3.1.md:28)). The measured consequence is direct: P1 height `10056px` in v2 versus `19066px` in Strata; rankSeparate only reduces it to `13761px`, still 37% taller ([W3 P1](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/docs/strata-view-w3-battery-report.md:40), [W4 §2](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/docs/strata-view-w4-extent-report.md:57)). W4 itself says the residual comes from provider/account full-width bands and subnet de-band may not touch it ([W4 §4](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/docs/strata-view-w4-extent-report.md:100)). OD-15 is aimed below the structural source.

4. **[P1] K=4 has no adequacy evidence, and the Forster upgrade rationale overstates its paper.** The battery compares only K=0 and K=4. K=4 cuts P1 compact crossings `273→136` and p50 `9665→2529`, but leaves p90 `13467` versus the `7098` baseline; there is no K curve, convergence run, constrained-sifting arm, or oracle gap ([W3 §2](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/docs/strata-view-w3-battery-report.md:40)). Standard layer sweeps normally continue until no improvement; global sifting experimentally dominated layer-by-layer barycenter/median for three or more layers (`forward-10-1007-3-540-46648-7-22`, pp. 2, 6–8). Exact one-sided crossing solutions were practical up to roughly 60 movable vertices, and exact/heuristic comparisons expose large local-optimum gaps (`doi-10-7155-jgaa-00001`, pp. 4–13)—larger than Strata’s stated single-digit-to-tens hull sizes. The “mostly <1%-from-optimal” description of Forster 2004 is also wrong: its experiments compare against another penalty-graph heuristic, reporting mostly <1% and at worst 3% difference, not distance to an optimum (`forward-10-1007-978-3-540-31843-9-22`, pp. 3, 8–10). Forster remains a valid constrained-ordering candidate, but not the cited quality certificate.

5. **[P1] `rankSeparate` and network simplex are not algorithmically incompatible; only the current sequential composition is.** Graphviz `ranksep` is merely minimum physical spacing between adjacent ranks, not a height/ranking algorithm ([Graphviz documentation](https://graphviz.org/docs/attrs/ranksep/)). Strata’s similarly named feature instead adds sibling-separation precedence edges and runs longest-path ranking. Gansner’s network-simplex formulation minimizes weighted edge span subject to arbitrary per-edge minimum-length constraints `λ(w)−λ(v)≥δ(e)` (`gansner-tse93`, pp. 6–8). Therefore the real dependency edges and separation edges can be solved together: give real edges their semantic weights, separation edges the required `δ`, and zero or policy-specific objective weight. The v1 experiment showing sequential NS destroys rankSeparate’s X-disjointness and adds 149% height proves that NS over the original graph is wrong; it does not prove constrained NS over the augmented graph is impossible ([v1 RFC DI-NS-4](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/docs/pipeline-rcll-layout-design.md:52)). W4’s “helps only with K=4” result is a genuine phase interaction, but the current mutual-exclusion rule prematurely rejects the standard joint formulation.

6. **[P2] The v1 port bookkeeping conflates standard phases with ad-hoc refinements.** Network-simplex `shorten` is standard layer assignment, correctly grounded in Gansner. `coordRepack` is not Brandes–Köpf coordinate assignment: it permutes nodes after ordering, brute-forces columns up to eight nodes, then performs coordinate descent. Its own record reports `−32.9%` intra-container ΔY but global crossings `367→371` and a `+0.6%` non-target regression ([v1 RFC M5r](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/docs/pipeline-rcll-layout-design.md:51)). Full Brandes–Köpf preserves the established order and performs alignment/compaction; the size-aware extension handles variable cards (`elk-10-1007-3-540-45848-4-3`, `doi-10-1007-978-3-319-27261-0-12`). Not porting coordRepack wholesale was correct. The papers chart is stale where it attributes “recursive container nesting” to Sander and treats Forster’s constrained global method as merely the running barycenter proposal ([papers chart R6/R7](/Users/tusharsariya/Projects/excalidraw-tf/docs/rcll-papers-reference-chart.md:27)). OD-9 ports/routing is literature-standard and preferable to radial layout for strict left-to-right flow, but bends and crossing angles require task-based evaluation before defaulting it.

## Verdict

**GO-WITH-CHANGES.** The global left-to-right layered substrate, global leaf ranking, derived compound spans, deterministic cycle repair, and eventual port-aware routing are literature-grounded. The current optimization direction is not: its critical tail gate computes the mean, its full-width bands are a project-specific structure already causing the measured residual, K=4 has no adequacy baseline, constrained network simplex was dismissed without testing the correct formulation, and the roadmap omits the empirically stronger interaction lever for impact tracing. Keep Strata; change the evaluation and next milestones before further de-band work.

## What I would change

1. Fix §2.5 to bootstrap the selected statistic—p50 or p90—not the mean. Re-run W3/W4 and withdraw “statistical parity” until the corrected p90 CI is known.
2. Move impact-trace interaction ahead of OD-15: select/hover a resource, highlight transitive upstream and downstream paths distinctly, dim unrelated elements, expose hop limits, and preserve direction.
3. Add a task gate: tracing time, wrong turns, and accuracy on frozen Terraform dependency questions. Add proxies for path continuity, path-specific crossings, crossing angle, edge-node interference, and branching ambiguity; demote raw vertical extent to compactness/supporting evidence.
4. Replace the subnet-only OD-15 plan with an A/B at the actual structural source: provider/account partial de-band versus global hierarchy-constrained ordering without full-width bands.
5. Replace “K=4 is the algorithm” with bounded adaptive optimization: constrained Forster/sifting until local convergence, plus exact/ILP or exhaustive oracles for small hulls. Report optimality gaps and a K/time/churn curve.
6. Test augmented network simplex over real dependency edges plus separation constraints before retaining permanent NS suppression.
7. Correct the papers chart; keep coordRepack classified as an ad-hoc v1 heuristic, and evaluate full size-aware BK or orthogonal ports only against the task gate.
