# Agent 5 — Crossing Minimization & Within-Layer Ordering (P2)

Charter: the rank-15 fan-in column where api6/api7 SSM params are dumped at the bottom while their sources sit high-Y one rank deeper (r13), so their edges rise diagonally across api2–5's edges → crossings. ROBUST / GENERIC / LITERATURE-BACKED.

Ground-truth of strata's ordering was re-verified against source (`terraformPipelineStrataOrdering.ts`), not assumed. **The single most load-bearing code fact:** strata does NOT run a textbook per-rank Sugiyama sweep. It lifts each E′ edge directly to the (source-unit, target-unit) pair (`liftStrataEdgesToUnits`, L96) — **no dummy-node chains** — and orders each hull as ONE linear unit sequence via a barycenter whose neighbour coordinate is the neighbour's **index in that single hull-wide sequence** (`applyStrataSweep`, L344–390: `sum += indexOf.get(inc.other)`). Down-sweep uses IN-neighbours, up-sweep OUT, plus same-layer connected neighbours. That one design choice is the root of P2.

---

## Q1 — Is this crossing EXPECTED given barycenter/median + sifting? Mechanism.

**Yes, expected — and for TWO compounding, well-documented reasons.**

**(a) Barycenter/median is a local heuristic on an NP-complete problem; it lands in local minima that a single-vertex move cannot escape.** Within-rank crossing minimisation is NP-complete even for two layers (Garey–Johnson via Eades–Wormald; restated in Gansner et al. 1993, `gansner-tse93` p.13: _"minimizing edge crossings in layouts of ranked graphs is NP-complete, even for only two ranks [EMW]"_, and Jünger–Mutzel 1996, `doi-10-1007-bfb0021817`). Barycenter and median are the standard weighting heuristics (Sugiyama–Tagawa–Toda 1981; Gansner p.13–14, `gansner-tse93:8`). Crucially, Gansner's dot does NOT trust median alone — after each `wmedian` sweep it runs a `transpose` (adjacent-exchange) pass **because median provably leaves crossings that a single swap removes** (`gansner-tse93` §3, `wmedian`+`transpose`, `gansner-tse93:9`). A stranded pair like api6/api7 is the canonical residue that transpose exists to remove.

**(b) The specific inversion is manufactured by strata's linearised barycenter, which conflates RANK (X) with within-rank order (Y).** Standard Sugiyama orders each rank _separately_, taking a unit's median over the **within-rank positions of its neighbours in the ADJACENT rank only** (Gansner p.13; Eiglsperger–Siebenhaller–Kaufmann `jgaa-2804…` §layer-ordering: _"the ordering of the vertices within adjacent layers Lᵢ₋₁ and Lᵢ determines the number of edge crossings"_). Strata instead scores a degree-1 sink by its single source's **index in the whole-hull sequence**. Because that sequence spans all ranks, api1–5's sources (r11) and api6/api7's sources (r13) sit at different sequence indices driven by rank depth + the alphabetical seed, NOT by the sources' actual Y. So the r15 column is sorted by "how deep/late my source is in the global sequence," which for api6/api7 (deeper rank, later seed key) forces them to the bottom even though their sources are high-Y. The diagonal rise and the crossings over api2–5 follow deterministically. This is not a bug in the barycenter math (the rational cross-multiply at L378 is exact); it is the barycenter being fed a **1-D coordinate that mixes the two axes** a proper layered ordering keeps separate.

**Does the multi-layer edge cause it (your dummy-chain hypothesis)?** Related but the opposite of what the framing assumed. In a _textbook_ engine the r13→r15 edge would be split into an r14 dummy (Sander 1996, `sander-compound-directed-graphs` §5 _"split long edges … into sequences of edge segments and dummy nodes"_), and that dummy would participate in the r14 ordering, propagating the source's Y down the column at every rank so the sink lands beside it. **Strata creates no such dummy** — the lifted edge is a single (r13-source → r15-sink) pair. So the far source IS seen (good), but only as one endpoint scored by the conflated sequence index (bad). The absence of the dummy chain removes the per-rank Y-propagation that would normally keep the fan-in aligned. So: expected, and the mechanism is _linearised-barycenter local minimum_, aggravated by _no dummy decomposition of multi-rank edges_.

## Q2 — Standard remedies and where strata stops short.

- **Iterated up/down sweep to convergence + keep-best.** Standard (Gansner §3: alternate top-down / bottom-up by iteration parity, keep the best ordering seen). Strata runs K=4 chained sweeps and, at the frozen config (`packedScoring=1`), keeps snapshots and lets the A0 whole-layout scorer pick (`strataPackedCandidateSequences`, L438, chains UNCONDITIONALLY then scores). So it _does_ keep-best — but only over the 4 barycenter snapshots, none of which generate the aligned order (see Q1: barycenter can't, it's a local min). More sweeps do **not** help — barycenter is idempotent at a fixed point.
- **The SIFTING algorithm (Matuszewski–Schönfeld–Molitor 1999, `forward-10-1007-3-540-46648-7-22`; sped up by Günther et al. 2001, `openalex-10-1007-3-540-44541-2-24`).** Sifting moves ONE vertex through _every_ position in its layer and keeps the best — a strictly stronger local move than a swap. Strata's `strataPackedCandidateSequences` single-unit sift clause (L599–660, _"insert each eligible unit at every position"_) **is** Matuszewski-style sifting, plus a group-sift clause. **But it is opt-in (`strataSiftRelocate`, default OFF) and, when enabled, the memoised evidence says the api6/api7 cases did not move** (see strata-sift-relocate-build memory). Two literature-consistent reasons it stops short: (i) sifting is scored by strata's crossing proxy, and the objective audit found the **chord-crossing proxy is inverted / blind** to exactly these moves — a correct sifting run scored by a _wrong_ counter relocates nothing; (ii) single-vertex sifting still cannot perform the simultaneous 2-vertex exchange that a pair like {api6,api7} vs {api2..5} sometimes needs — that is transpose's job, and strata has **no transpose / adjacent-exchange pass**. So the gap is: strata has median-sweep + sifting but is **missing (a) the transpose escape and (b) a truthful crossing score**, which is the precise pair Gansner ships together.
- **Global vs layer-local.** Strata is neither clean layer-local (it linearises the whole hull) nor global-exact; the polyhedral/exact route (Jünger–Lee–Mutzel–Odenthal 1997, `doi-10-1007-3-540-63938-1-46`) is impractical here. The robust middle is the classic local: per-rank median + transpose to convergence.

## Q3 — Ordering to reduce EDGE LENGTH (sink near source Y), not just crossings.

P2's deeper want ("api6/api7 params near their sources' Y") is a **straightness / vertical-deviation** objective, addressed by the _coordinate_ phase, not only ordering:

- **Brandes–Köpf "Fast and Simple Horizontal Coordinate Assignment" (2002, `elk-10-1007-3-540-45848-4-3`; erratum `forward-10-48550-arxiv-2008-01252`).** Aligns each node to its **median neighbour** (absolute-difference objective ⇒ median, not barycenter). A **degree-1 sink aligns EXACTLY to its single source** — precisely the "sink at source Y" P2 wants. Strata's `refineStrataVerticalSlots` (median-target Y within rank) is a bounded analog, but it is **order-blocked**: it can slide a sink toward its source's Y only until it would collide with a slot neighbour — it cannot reorder past api1–5. So Y-refine is _necessary but not sufficient_; the ORDER must be fixed first. (And packed dropY-skyline can override Y entirely — P5.)
- **Weighted median (Gansner p.14, `gansner-tse93:9`)** already biases a node toward the median of its neighbours — the right ordering key for a fan-in column, if computed on true adjacent-rank Y rather than the conflated sequence index.
- **Minimum-edge-length coordinate solves:** Gansner's network-simplex X-solve and the MCF flow formulation (Jünger–Mutzel–Spisla 2018, `forward-10-1007-978-3-030-04414-5-13`) minimise total edge length exactly given a fixed order — again downstream of order.

## Q4 — Ranked ROBUST fixes for P2.

**FIX 1 (most robust; fits strata as an added pass). Per-rank weighted-median re-order + a transpose/adjacent-exchange pass, iterated to convergence, scored by TRUE crossings.** Gansner GKNV93 (`gansner-tse93`, `wmedian`+`transpose`). Re-key the within-rank order by the median of each unit's in-neighbours' _within-rank_ Y-order in the adjacent rank (not the whole-hull sequence index), then run transpose: for each adjacent pair in the column, swap iff it strictly lowers _rendered_ segment-intersection crossings; repeat until a full sweep makes no swap. Transpose is the specific mechanism that removes the api6/api7 local minimum barycenter/sift leave. **Robust:** provably targets exactly this crossing class, O(rank²) per rank, deterministic, LR-safe (same rank ⇒ same X ⇒ no backward edges), and it also cuts edge length because lifting the sinks to their sources' band shortens the diagonal chords. **Edge-case risk:** must score by true intersections, NOT the inverted chord proxy (else it relocates nothing, like the current sift); and it must score the WHOLE column (api6/api7 vs the P1 DLQs sharing r15) so it doesn't trade one crossing for another. **Requires-replacing-sift? No** — it's an added transpose pass plus a corrected median key; strata's sift can stay.

**FIX 2 (robust, attacks the root; more invasive). Decompose multi-rank E′ edges into per-rank dummy units before ordering.** Sander 1996 (`sander-compound-directed-graphs` §5) / Sugiyama STT81. Give the r13→r15 edge an r14 virtual unit so the column ordering "sees" the source's Y at every intervening rank and propagates it down the fan-in. **Robust & generic** — it makes strata a proper layered engine and fixes the whole class, not just api6/api7. **Cost:** touches ranking + placement + routing (dummies must be threaded and later collapsed); larger blast radius; **requires reworking the lift**, so higher risk than Fix 1. Best as the eventual correct-architecture move, not the surgical P2 fix.

**FIX 3 (coordinate-side complement, not standalone). Brandes–Köpf median Y-alignment as the Y-assignment for degree-1 sinks, unblocked from slot order.** `elk-10-1007-3-540-45848-4-3`. Align each fan-in sink to its source's Y. **Only works once Fix 1 has corrected the order** (else order-blocked, as `refineStrataVerticalSlots` already is), and packed dropY must not override it. Necessary-but-not-sufficient; ship with Fix 1, not instead of it.

**Ranking:** Fix 1 ≫ Fix 3 (dependent) ; Fix 2 is the strategic root fix but out of scope for a surgical P2 change.

## SELF-ADVERSARIAL

- **Genuine algorithmic gap, or just under-iterating?** GENUINE. Strata already runs K=4 sweeps + Matuszewski sifting + a group-sift + a relocate operator, and the frozen config still leaves api6/api7 inverted, and the sift-relocate memory records the owner cases _did not move_. Barycenter is idempotent at its fixed point, so **more sweeps are provably free-of-effect**. The missing primitive is **transpose (pairwise exchange)**, which barycenter/median cannot emulate and single-vertex sifting only partially covers — this is exactly why Gansner ships transpose alongside wmedian. So it is not under-iteration; it is a missing operator + a mis-scored existing one.
- **Would fix 1 relocate nothing, like the current sift?** RISK REAL. If transpose is scored by the same chord-crossing proxy the objective audit found inverted, it fails identically. Fix 1 is only robust if scored on **rendered segment intersections**. This is the single biggest threat to the fix and must be stated as a precondition, not a footnote. (It also aligns with the readability-synthesis verdict that the machinery _can_ adopt once the score is truthful.)
- **Does fixing P2 order perturb P1/P3?** Bounded. The re-order is _within rank 15_, so X is untouched → LR preserved, no backward edges (contra the xcompact per-hull failure, which was an X move). But rank 15 also holds the P1 DLQs; lifting api6/api7 pushes other members down and could lengthen a DLQ edge or shift its crossings. Mitigation: score the whole column, not the api6/api7 pair — which is why Fix 1 is a column-global median+ transpose, not a targeted 2-swap. P3/P4 are different ranks/regions and are untouched by a rank-15-local re-order. My fix does NOT touch X-packing, so the xcompact-removed findings do not apply.
- **Is "shared source" wrong (as the crystallization says)?** Confirmed: each param is a degree-1 sink of its OWN compute; it is fan-IN of independent single-source sinks. That strengthens Fix 1/Fix 3 — a degree-1 sink's median is exactly its one source, so the correct order/Y is unambiguous and cheap.

## TIGHT SUMMARY

**Is the P2 crossing expected? YES.** It is a textbook barycenter/median local minimum that a single-vertex move cannot escape — Gansner ships a `transpose` (adjacent-exchange) pass precisely to remove this residue (`gansner-tse93` §3), and strata has median-sweeps + sifting but **no transpose**. It is aggravated by strata's non-standard ordering: it scores a degree-1 sink by its source's index in the _whole-hull_ sequence (L344–390), which **conflates rank (X) with within-rank Y**, so api6/api7's deeper-rank sources force their sinks to the bottom of the column regardless of the sources' actual high Y. It is NOT under-iteration (barycenter is idempotent; K=4 already runs; sift already exists).

**Single most robust ordering fix:** a **per-rank weighted-median re-order + a transpose/adjacent-exchange pass iterated to convergence, keyed on adjacent-rank Y and scored on TRUE rendered crossings** (Gansner, Koutsofios, North & Vo 1993, `gansner-tse93`, DOI-less LNCS / graphviz TSE93). It is LR-safe (within-rank, X-preserving), also shortens the fan-in edges, fits strata as an added pass without replacing sift — with the hard precondition that it be scored by real segment intersections, not the inverted chord proxy, or it will relocate nothing exactly as the current sift did.
